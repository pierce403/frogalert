## Failed entry attempts

The OEM application repeatedly enumerated as `0416:5020`, manufacturer
`wch.cn`, product `CH583`. Bridging C3 while holding the side button nearest
USB caused USB disconnect/re-enumeration but always returned to that
application. No `4348:55e0` or `1a86:55e0` ISP identity appeared. This proves
that C3 reset the target; it does not prove a broken bootloader.

## Position correction

Nyx's revision-specific flashing page identifies KEY2 on `B1144C_260404` as
the side button **farther from USB**. The earlier `B1144C_250901` physical
session used KEY2 nearest USB. Both map KEY2 electrically to active-low PB22,
but the physical switch position is revision-specific.

## Successful entry and open-firmware boot

With the farther-from-USB KEY2 held, momentarily bridging C3 enumerated
`4348:55e0` twice: at 16:19:10 and 16:19:42 local time on 2026-07-28. At
16:20:07 the application enumerated as `0416:5020`, manufacturer
`FOSSASIA WAS HERE`, product `LED Badge Magic`, serial
`BM1144-C fw: v0.1`, with HID and CDC ACM (`ttyACM0`) interfaces.

This verifies the physical ISP-entry method on `B1144C_260404` and that a
FOSSASIA application booted. It does not identify the programmed artifact:
there is no captured `wchisp` program/verify command or hash-bound transcript.
The public site therefore keeps C3 out of its routine checklist, and every
`260404` FrogAlert candidate remains hardware-unverified.
