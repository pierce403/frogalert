Why does original or unknown firmware need expert recovery?

On the confirmed USB-C B1144C_250901 board, holding its KEY2—the button nearest USB—while pressing the populated RESET switch did not enter ISP. Its documented first entry required a qualified operator to hold KEY2 while momentarily bridging both ends of PCB capacitor C3. Nyx documents the same C3 operation for B1144C_260404, but with KEY2 explicitly identified as the button farther from USB; FrogAlert has not yet captured a successful ISP enumeration for that revision. This hazardous rail-collapse maneuver is not routine, is not a battery operation, and is deliberately not implemented as a website checklist. Leave the soldered cell and its leads alone. Review the hardware evidence and safety boundary and Nyx's revision-specific notes before qualified bench work.

If programming is interrupted

Do not claim success. Keep the exact selected BIN and its hash, disconnect power, repeat the KEY2 recovery sequence, establish a fresh read-only identity, then retry the complete write and verification flow. A replacement USB device must never inherit an active flash session.

Browser and operating-system USB support varies. A missing permission prompt or driver conflict is not proof that badge firmware is broken.
